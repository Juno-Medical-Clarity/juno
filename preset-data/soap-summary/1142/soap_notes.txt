Subjective:
- Loud and obnoxious snoring
- Wakes up partner due to snoring
- Uses CPAP due to almost severe sleep apnea
- CPAP has reduced snoring but mask pops off during the night
- Wears CPAP 5-7 nights a week; mask falls off or is taken off randomly at night
- Partner reminds patient to put the CPAP back on
- Sleep pattern: Goes to bed around 11 or 11:30 PM, wakes up at 6 or 7 AM on weekends; sleeps in until 8 or 9 AM on weekdays; may sleep around midnight on Saturdays
- Feels good upon waking, less tossing and turning, sheets remain on the bed
- Lost 15-18 pounds due to exercising and dieting
- Motivated to stop snoring
- Works at Smith Barney, from 8 or 8:30 AM to 5 or 5:30 PM
- Does not feel stressed; able to stay awake through shifts
- Experiences some fatigue during hay fever season, but it is rare
- Relevant medical history: Sleep apnea, hay fever

Objective:
- CPAP set to 10 cm H2O nasal pressure
- Predominantly suffers from hypopnea
- Notable weight loss: 15-18 pounds

Assessment:
- Sleep apnea
- Hypopnea
- Possible issues with CPAP compliance due to mask fit or usage

Plan:
- Continue using CPAP as prescribed
- Consider evaluation of mask fit and options for alternative masks to improve compliance
- Monitor weight loss progress and its impact on sleep apnea
- Discuss management strategies for hay fever to reduce fatigue symptoms
- Follow-up appointment to reassess sleep quality and effectiveness of CPAP therapy
