# SOAP Notes — Sample 0579

Subjective:
- Patient is a 14-year-old female.
- Chief complaint: Left knee pain ("hurts really bad").
- Duration: Pain has been present since a fall in November 2007.
- Associated symptoms: Pain worsened after physical therapy (PT).
- Relevant medical history: MRI indicated left patellar chondromalacia.
- Family history: Not mentioned.
- Allergies: Not mentioned.
- Concerns: Patient expresses a desire to return to playing soccer and is concerned about the severity of her pain and the effectiveness of PT.

Objective:
- MRI report shows left patellar chondromalacia.
- Patient has been wearing a knee brace as recommended.

Assessment:
- Left patellar chondromalacia.
- Severe pain not improved with physical therapy.
- Consideration of surgical intervention (lateral capsular release).

Plan:
- Discussed the option of lateral capsular release surgery to address the partially dislocating kneecap.
- Patient and family expressed willingness to proceed with surgery.
- Postoperative plan includes the need for physical therapy after surgery.
- Discussed risks of surgery: infection (less than 1% risk), bleeding, changes in sensation and motion, failure to relieve pain, need for postoperative rehab, and significant postoperative swelling.
