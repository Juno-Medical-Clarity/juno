# SOAP Notes — Sample 1368
# Dataset: https://huggingface.co/datasets/SubashNeupane/dataset_SOAP_summary

Subjective:
- Patient is a 44-year-old female aircraft mechanic.
- Injury occurred while working on an airplane; fell between the plane and a stand.
- Injury to the left knee with history of two arthroscopies and a patellar replacement, followed by a left knee replacement.
- MRI report from September 2006 shows nonspecific edema anterior to the patellar tendon; no evidence of fracture or ligamentous avulsion. 
- Knee pain located in the front and sides of the knee, radiating down the femur and tibia to the back of the thigh, with numbness and tingling.
- Reports lower back pain, unsure if related to knee injury.
- Pain worsens with walking, described as constant, shooting, cramping, aching, throbbing, sharp, and stabbing.
- Pain intensity: average 6/10, worst 10/10, least 3/10.
- Pain wakes her at night occasionally.
- Pain somewhat relieved by lying down or elevating the leg.
- Allergic to many pain medications.
- Changed walking pattern to reduce weight on the knee, possibly contributing to back pain.

Objective:
- MRI from September 2006 shows nonspecific edema anterior to the patellar tendon; no acute fracture or ligament abnormalities.
- No abnormal discomfort or pain over the rest of the knee upon examination.
- Patient feels numbness, tingling, and pins and needles sensation in the knee and thigh.
- Reports instability in the knee with associated pain.

Assessment:
- Left knee pain with history of multiple surgeries.
- Possible post-surgical complications (edema, instability, neuropathic symptoms in thigh).
- Potential relationship between altered gait and lower back pain.
- Differential diagnoses may include post-operative complications, patellofemoral pain syndrome, or possible nerve involvement.

Plan:
- Review additional MRI reports.
- Consider referral for physical therapy focusing on knee strength and gait training.
- Discuss pain management strategies that do not involve medications to which the patient is allergic.
- Schedule follow-up appointment to monitor progress and reassess pain management strategies.
