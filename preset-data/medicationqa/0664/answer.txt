Normally when you swallow, the muscles in the esophagus move in waves to push food down into your stomach. Just after the food enters the stomach, a band of muscle (the lower esophageal sphincter, or LES) closes off the esophagus. If the muscle does not close tightly enough or gets weak over time, stomach contents can back up into the esophagus, causing heartburn and other symptoms. || Some medicines can also weaken the LES. Among them are: || Anticholinergic drugs

Benztropine (Cogentin)
Dicyclomine (Bentyl)
Hyoscyamine (Levsin)
Isopropamide
Scopolamine
