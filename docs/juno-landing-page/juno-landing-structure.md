## Section-by-Section Breakdowns

### 1. voyagehealth.ai — single-audience baseline (useful for core one-pager skeleton)

1. **Nav** — Features / How It Works / About / Sign in / Get Started (sticky, simple).
2. **Hero** — Eyebrow "AI-Powered Health Intelligence Platform" → H1 "Your Health Journey, AI-Powered." → one-sentence subhead → two CTAs (primary "Start Free Today", secondary "See How It Works") → trust strip directly under CTAs: "HIPAA Compliant · Free to Use · 4.9/5 Rating" (three inline badges, no logos).
3. **Features grid** — H2 "Everything you need for better health" + subhead. 6 feature cards, icon + 3-6 word title + 1-sentence description each (AI-Powered Insights, Guided Health Conversations, Personalized Roadmaps, Privacy First, Smart Reminders, Longitudinal Tracking).
4. **How It Works** — H2 "Get started in minutes" + subhead. 4 numbered steps (01-04), each a short title + 1-sentence description. Linear, no branching by audience (single audience only).
5. **About** — H2 "Healthcare meets artificial intelligence" narrative paragraph (mission/credibility) + 3 stat tiles (Privacy-First AI, Free Health Guidance, 99.9% Uptime) + a product screenshot/chat mockup showing the AI in action (concrete, not abstract).
6. **Final CTA band** — H2 "Ready to take control of your health?" + 1 line + primary CTA button + small "already have an account? sign in" link.
7. **Footer** — logo, nav repeat, copyright. Minimal — no compliance links beyond the earlier HIPAA badge.

**Takeaway:** clean 5-section skeleton (hero → features → how-it-works → proof/about → CTA) works well as the spine even for multi-audience pages — the audience-differentiation just has to be layered into features/proof sections rather than replacing them.

### 2. includedhealth.com — audience split via top-level toggle (contrast case, NOT a one-pager)

- **Nav pattern**: top-of-page toggle **"For Members" / "For Organizations"** — this switches the entire site context, not a same-page section. "Organizations" further branches into Employers / Health Plans / Public Sector / Labor Unions / Benefit Consultants as separate landing pages.
- Homepage itself (under "For Members") is single-audience: hero ("Included Health is designed to treat you better") → capability carousel (5 slides: Personalized care, Billing advocacy, Second opinions, 24/7 support, In-person/virtual care) → "Included Health is designed for [when your last visit left you with questions]" segment bridging to org signup ("offered by leading employers and health plans as a covered benefit") → approach statement + product video → testimonial carousel (14 quotes, 4.9/5 stat) → FAQ accordion → final CTA → footer with solutions/who-we-serve link columns + compliance links (Notice of Nondiscrimination) + legal.
- **Takeaway (contrast):** this is the "separate pages per audience" strategy — appropriate for a company with deep content per audience, but it fragments the pitch and requires visitors to self-select before seeing any value prop. **Not the model for Juno**, since Juno explicitly wants all three audiences convinced from one URL. Worth citing as what to avoid: don't force an early either/or fork that hides the other audiences' value.

### 3. getgarner.com (Garner Health) — compact multi-audience card block on ONE homepage (closest structural match)

1. **Nav** — Employers / Advisors / Health Plans / Providers / Members / Resources / Company (nav still lists all audiences but homepage doesn't fork).
2. **Announcement bar** — funding/credibility ticker ("Garner has raised $100M...") — optional pattern, low priority for Juno.
3. **Hero** — H1 "Top Providers. Bottom line results." (short, benefit-forward, works for multiple audiences at once) + single CTA "Request a demo".
4. **Audience card row (KEY PATTERN)** — four compact blocks side by side, one per audience, each with: bolded audience label ("For Individuals & Families", "For Employers", "For Advisors", "For Providers") + 1-sentence benefit description each. No icons, no separate CTA per card — just labeled paragraphs in a row. Immediately followed by one social-proof line: "700 organizations partner with Garner..." (a single quantified trust stat, not logos).
5. **Core data/product story** — H2 "The next revolution in healthcare data" — explains the underlying asset (60B medical records / 320M patients) that makes the product credible, i.e., the "how it works" section doubles as the trust/differentiation section for a data-driven product.
6. **Feature explainer blocks** — 3 blocks, each pairing a headline + description with an implied UI callout (Garner Assistant, reimbursement, provider recommendations) — alternating text/visual rhythm rather than a flat grid.
7. **Testimonial wall** — large scrolling grid of member quotes (name + city/state), high volume rather than curated — signals "real, many users," useful for consumer trust.
8. **Resources teaser** — 3-4 content cards (whitepaper/podcast/webinar) — optional, low priority for a v1 one-pager.
9. **Final CTA** — "Create your free account" / "Give your employees the best care" (dual CTA phrased per two audiences) + newsletter signup.
10. **Footer** — nav columns split by audience (For Members / Solutions: Employers, Advisors, Health Plans, Providers) + company + resources + legal (Terms, Privacy, CA Privacy Notice).

**Takeaway:** the four-audience segmentation happens in ONE compact horizontal block near the top (not tabs, not full sections each) — each audience gets a label + one sentence, nothing more. Depth is deferred to nav-linked subpages. This is the pattern to imitate at v1 scope for Juno's three audiences.

### 4. waymarkcare.com — three-audience cards + evidence/stats section + audience-segmented final CTA (strongest match for a *clinical/health-data* product)

1. **Nav** — Services / Locations / Partners (Health Plans, Health Systems, Community Partners) / Technology / Research / Contact.
2. **Hero** — short mission line ("Better care starts with community") + immediate partner-logo row ("A few of our trusted partners") — logos placed very early, right under the hero, not buried lower on the page.
3. **What we do** — plain-language description of the service model + "See all services" link + role list (Community Health Workers, Care Coordinators, Pharmacy Teams, Licensed Counselors) — humanizes the product before it pitches audiences.
4. **Audience card row (KEY PATTERN, 3-way)** — "For partners: Prevent health crises before they happen" framing, then three cards: **Health Plans** ("Achieve sustainable cost-savings through targeted early interventions"), **Health Systems** ("Make your Medicaid population more sustainable to serve"), **Community Partners** ("Reach more Medicaid patients in your community") — each is a one-line value statement tied to that stakeholder's own incentive (cost, sustainability, reach) rather than a generic feature list restated three times.
5. **How it works / technology** — "Predict who needs help. Intervene before it's too late" — explains the tech/data engine in plain terms, "physician-supervised and validated through peer-reviewed research" (credibility phrase embedded directly in the how-it-works copy).
6. **Evidence/stats section (KEY PATTERN for health data trust)** — H2 "Built on evidence, proven by outcomes" — **4 hard numbers in a stat-tile row**: 48% reduction in avoidable hospitalizations, 20% reduction in avoidable ER visits, $2,347 savings PMPY, 3:1 ROI — each with a citation line ("Source: NEJM Catalyst (2024). DOI: ..."). This is the single strongest "why should a payer/health system trust this" section across all sites studied — outcome numbers + peer-reviewed citation, not vague claims.
7. **Research/citations list** — 2-3 named academic papers with author/year, reinforcing rigor (works well for a payer/clinical audience specifically, less relevant to patients).
8. **Press/news logos** — outlet names (FierceHealthcare, Becker's, Yahoo Finance, MedCity News, Healthcare Brew) each linked to a real article — third-party validation distinct from testimonials.
9. **Care team section** — named staff with title + location — humanizes the org, unusual but effective trust signal for a care-coordination product.
10. **Final CTA — segmented by audience (KEY PATTERN)** — "Let's talk" framed as three literal sub-paths: "Patients: check eligibility or ask questions" (phone/email), "Health Plans & Health Systems: learn how Waymark can support your Medicaid population" (partnerships email) — i.e., the CTA itself branches by audience instead of using one generic button, because the next action really differs per audience (patient wants to check eligibility; payer wants a partnership conversation).
11. **Footer** — standard nav + **compliance-specific links**: HIPAA Notice of Privacy Practices, Non-Discrimination Notice, Compliance and Ethics Hotline — beyond generic Privacy/Terms, this is health-data-specific footer content worth copying.

**Takeaway:** Waymark is the best model for a clinical/data platform serving payers + providers + patients on one page — three-way audience cards tied to each stakeholder's own incentive, a dedicated evidence/outcomes section with citations (critical for payer credibility), and a CTA that branches by audience rather than forcing one generic action.

## Recommended Structure for the Juno One-Pager

**Segmentation approach: weave-plus-one-card-row, not full audience-tabbed sections.**
Rationale: every multi-audience comparable that stayed a true one-pager (Garner, Waymark) used a single compact, side-by-side card block to say "here's why this matters to *you* specifically" early on — then let the rest of the page (features, evidence, proof) speak generally, with audience-specific callouts woven in only where the underlying fact genuinely differs (e.g., an outcome stat that's payer-relevant, a workflow detail that's clinic-relevant). Full tabbed or triple-repeated sections (features-for-clinics / features-for-payers / features-for-patients, three separate times) were not observed anywhere and would roughly triple page length for marginal gain. includedhealth.com's nav-toggle "choose your audience first" pattern was ruled out because it forces visitors to self-select before seeing any shared value prop — wrong for a one-page/one-URL brief.

### Section-by-section spec

1. **Nav (sticky)** — Logo · Features · How It Works · Evidence/Results · Contact/Demo CTA. Keep nav single, not audience-forked (per includedhealth.com contrast finding).

2. **Hero**
   - Purpose: state the core value prop in a way that's true for all three audiences simultaneously (per Garner's "Top Providers. Bottom line results." pattern — short, outcome-not-feature).
   - Content needed: 1 eyebrow line (category label), 1 H1 (≤8 words, audience-neutral benefit), 1 subhead sentence, 1 primary CTA ("Request a demo" / "Get started" — generic, not audience-forked here), 1 inline trust strip directly beneath (e.g., "HIPAA-compliant" + 1-2 more badges, mirroring voyagehealth.ai's hero trust-badge placement).

3. **Partner/pilot logo row** (if any exist yet — clinics, health systems, or design partners)
   - Purpose: immediate third-party credibility, before any claims are made (per Waymark placing logos directly under the hero).
   - Content needed: 3-6 logos, greyscale, "Trusted by" or "Working with" label. If Juno has no logos yet at launch, skip rather than fake it — no comparable site fabricated proof it didn't have.

4. **Audience card row — "Built for Clinics / Payers / Patients"** (KEY SECTION)
   - Purpose: let each of the three visitors self-recognize their reason to care within 5 seconds, without forking the page.
   - Content needed per audience: 1 bolded label + 1 sentence value statement framed around *that audience's own incentive* (clinics: workflow/time saved; payers: cost/outcomes/risk; patients: access/trust/control) — mirror Waymark's incentive-specific one-liners, not generic feature restatement. Optionally 2-3 short bullets per audience if one sentence is too thin, but keep it under Garner/Waymark's brevity — this is a signpost, not a pitch.
   - Why it matters: this is the one structural element every true multi-audience one-pager in the research uses, and it's what makes a single URL work for three buyers.

5. **Core product/data story ("how it works" doubling as credibility)**
   - Purpose: explain what Juno actually does and why the underlying data/methodology can be trusted — for a clinical data platform this section carries real trust weight, not just UX steps (per Garner's "60B medical records" narrative and Waymark's "physician-supervised, peer-reviewed" phrase).
   - Content needed: 3-4 numbered or visual steps OR a short narrative paragraph + 1 supporting stat about the data asset itself (scale, validation method, or clinical oversight) + optionally one product screenshot/mockup (per voyagehealth.ai's chat-mockup visual).

6. **Feature grid**
   - Purpose: concretely show what the product does, general capabilities that ladder up to all three audiences.
   - Content needed: 4-6 feature cards, icon + 3-6 word title + 1-sentence description each (per voyagehealth.ai's grid format — most skimmable pattern observed). Where a feature is audience-specific, note it inline in the description ("...so clinics spend less time on X") rather than a separate grid per audience.

7. **Evidence/outcomes section** (KEY SECTION for payer credibility)
   - Purpose: give payers (and skeptical clinics) hard numbers, per Waymark's strongest-in-class pattern.
   - Content needed: 3-4 stat tiles (e.g., time saved, cost impact, accuracy/coverage metric, adoption metric) each with a one-line citation or methodology note if available. If Juno has no outcome data yet at launch, this section should either be omitted or replaced with a narrower "what we measure" framing rather than invented numbers.

8. **Social proof** (testimonial or case-study format — pick whichever is true)
   - Purpose: humanize the product for the patient audience especially, and validate for clinic/payer audiences via a case-study framing if testimonials aren't yet available.
   - Content needed: 3-6 quotes (name + role/org, e.g., "Dr. X, [Clinic]" or "Care Coordinator, [Health System]") OR 1 short case-study callout if testimonials don't exist yet. Avoid Garner-style mass-testimonial walls at launch — too much content for a v1 one-pager; 3-6 curated quotes is enough.

9. **Trust/compliance strip**
   - Purpose: reinforce security/compliance explicitly for a health-data product, beyond the hero badge.
   - Content needed: HIPAA compliance statement, security practice mention (encryption/SOC2 if applicable), and a short "how we handle data" line — this can be a compact strip (icons + short labels) rather than a full section, placed either right before the final CTA or in the footer (per Waymark's footer-level compliance links).

10. **Final CTA band**
    - Purpose: convert. Consider branching by audience here specifically, since the *next action* genuinely differs (clinic wants a demo, payer wants a partnership conversation, patient wants to check access/join) — mirrors Waymark's segmented "Let's talk" CTA, the one place segmentation should go deep.
    - Content needed: 1 H2 (audience-neutral closing line) + up to 3 CTA paths, one per audience, each a short label + contact method or form (e.g., "Clinics: Request a demo" / "Payers: Talk to partnerships" / "Patients: Get started").

11. **Footer**
    - Purpose: capture remaining nav depth and required legal/compliance content without cluttering the body.
    - Content needed: logo + tagline, nav link columns (optionally grouped by audience, per Garner/Waymark), contact info, legal links (Terms, Privacy Policy), health-specific compliance links (HIPAA Notice of Privacy Practices, Non-Discrimination Notice — per Waymark), social links, copyright line.

### What to explicitly avoid (anti-patterns from research)
- **Don't fork the whole page by audience at the top** (includedhealth.com's nav toggle) — it forces premature self-selection and hides value from the other two audiences on a page meant to serve all three at once.
- **Don't repeat full feature/benefit sections three times**, once per audience — no comparable did this; it roughly triples page length for a one-pager and dilutes the shared product story.
- **Don't publish a pricing table** — no B2B(2C) health comparable did; all gate to a demo/contact/eligibility CTA instead.
- **Don't fabricate stats, testimonials, or logos** if Juno doesn't have them yet — every section above should be *omitted* rather than filled with placeholder-flavored real claims until real data/quotes/partners exist.
